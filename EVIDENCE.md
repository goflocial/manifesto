# Appendix: the evidence

**Draft v0.1, October 2026.** The manifesto makes the case *for* flocial networks. This appendix holds the
evidence behind it. That means the public record that made this work urgent, the protections already agreed,
the research on what helps, and the full ledger of sources. Every factual claim in the Flocial Manifesto, Framework and Rubric cites
an entry here. If a claim has no entry, it doesn't go in.

- **Part A:** the public record
- **Part B:** the settlement floor
- **Part C:** what helps
- **Part D:** the ledger, E01–E57

## Part A: The public record

In 2026, for the first time, the design of major social platforms was tested in court. These are civil
findings and settlements, not criminal guilt, and a settlement is not an admission.

- **March 2026, Los Angeles.** A jury found Meta and YouTube **negligent in the design of their platforms**:
  that they knew the design was dangerous, failed to warn, and caused real harm to a young woman who started
  using them as a child. The features on trial were infinite scroll, autoplay, algorithmic recommendations,
  beauty filters and push notifications [[E01]](#E01). The judge declined to overturn the verdict, and Meta
  is appealing [[E02]](#E02).
- **March 2026, New Mexico.** A jury found that Meta **willfully** violated the state's consumer-protection law
  37,500 times, and **knowingly concealed** what it knew about the sexual exploitation of children on its
  platforms [[E03]](#E03).
- **August 2026, New Mexico.** A judge declared Meta a **public nuisance** and brought the penalties to
  **$942 million**, with design changes for minors [[E04]](#E04). Meta says it will appeal.
- **August 2026, more than 40 states.** Facing trial, Meta agreed to pay **up to $17 billion** and to redesign
  teen accounts, while denying wrongdoing. The settlement still needs court approval [[E05]](#E05).
- **School districts.** About 1,200 districts have sued, and the first case to reach trial was settled
  [[E07]](#E07).
- **The companies' own research.** An internal slide read "We make body image issues worse for one in three
  teen girls". Facebook noted the sample was small [[E09]](#E09). A 2020 study found people who stepped away
  from Facebook for a week reported less depression, anxiety and loneliness. Court filings allege it was
  halted, and Meta says its method was flawed [[E08]](#E08). Ranking once counted an emoji reaction as worth
  five likes [[E10]](#E10).
- **New forms of the same pattern.** AI "companion" companies have settled suits over harm to teenagers, and
  others are pending [[E15]](#E15) [[E16]](#E16). Subscriptions designed to be hard to cancel led to a
  $2.5 billion settlement [[E17]](#E17).

**The science is honestly debated.** Researchers still argue about how much average screen time affects the
average teenager [[E13]](#E13) [[E14]](#E14), and the US Surgeon General's position is that no one can yet
call social media sufficiently safe for children [[E11]](#E11). The manifesto doesn't rest on that debate. It
rests on design, which courts and the companies' own research have now examined directly.

## Part B: The settlement floor

To settle with the states, Meta agreed that teen accounts would get these protections [[E05]](#E05). A
flocial network offers all of them to everyone, by default, and without anyone being watched more closely to
get them [[E06]](#E06).

| Protection | In the settlement | In a flocial network |
|---|---|---|
| A daily time limit | Teens, in participating states | Feeds that end on their own |
| A feed not ranked by an algorithm | Teens | The default |
| Like counts hidden | Teens | No public counts at all |
| Quiet at night | Teens | Quiet by default |
| Muted notifications during school | Teens | Quiet by default |
| Autoplay can be turned off | Teens | No autoplay |
| A reminder after 15 minutes of scrolling | Teens | Nothing endless to scroll |
| No cosmetic-surgery filters | Teens | None |
| Private by default | Teens | Invite-only |
| Less exposure to suspicious adults | Teens | No contact from strangers |

## Part C: What helps

- **Close relationships:** strongly linked to survival and to healthy ageing [[E29]](#E29) [[E30]](#E30).
- **Composed messages from close ties** raise well-being more than one-click likes and passive scrolling
  [[E21]](#E21).
- **We have room for a few:** about 5 intimates and 15 close friends, online or off [[E31]](#E31).
- **Meaningful conversation** goes with happier days [[E48]](#E48).
- **A wide mix of social ties** goes with better resistance to everyday illness [[E47]](#E47).
- **Friendships across income lines** are among the strongest predictors of children's upward mobility [[E46]](#E46).
- **Visible community rules** raise newcomers' participation and cut harassment [[E50]](#E50).
- **Members helping govern** is what keeps shared spaces working [[E51]](#E51).
- **Fewer interruptions, and the right to switch off,** at work [[E53]](#E53) [[E54]](#E54).
- **Feeling safe to speak up** is what most sets effective teams apart [[E57]](#E57).
- **Ranking for agreement** in public consultation [[E55]](#E55), and **peers helping peers** in customer
  communities [[E56]](#E56).
- **Flourishing can be measured** across six domains, including close relationships [[E28]](#E28).
- **A pause before posting** reduces offensive messages, with lasting effect [[E22]](#E22).
- **Ranking for agreement across divides,** not for reaction [[E23]](#E23) [[E24]](#E24).
- **Design codes and frameworks** for healthier platforms: [[E20]](#E20) [[E25]](#E25) [[E26]](#E26)
  [[E27]](#E27).
- **Privacy as context** [[E32]](#E32), children's rights online [[E33]](#E33) [[E34]](#E34), and fair, shared

## Part D: The ledger

**Strength tiers.** Most decisive first:

| Tier | Kind | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **L** | Legal finding | A jury verdict or court judgment. Civil findings, not criminal guilt. |
| **S** | Settlement or enforcement | Terms a company agreed to. *A settlement is not an admission*, and Meta denies wrongdoing in each case below. |
| **D** | Internal document | A company's own research or communications, as disclosed in court or by a whistleblower. |
| **P** | Peer-reviewed or scholarly | Journal, conference or law-review publication. Preprints are marked as preprints. |
| **G** | Government or standard | Statute, regulation, official advisory or formal standard. |
| **R** | Reporting | Journalism describing events or documents. |
| **O** | Advocacy or practice | Frameworks, codes and opinion from organizations. Useful, but persuasive rather than proof. |

**Writing rules for anyone citing this ledger:**
- Say "found negligent", "found liable" or "settled", exactly as the entry says. Never "guilty".
- Note an appeal whenever one is pending.
- When the science is disputed, say so.

### E01 · K.G.M. v. Meta et al.: the first jury verdict on addictive design
- **Tier:** L
- **When and where:** 2026-03-25, Los Angeles Superior Court, JCCP 5255, first bellwether trial.
- **What it establishes:**
  - The jury found Meta and YouTube **negligent in the design** of their platforms.
  - It found they **knew the design was dangerous**, **failed to warn**, and caused substantial harm to the plaintiff.
  - The design was a substantial factor in her depression, anxiety and body dysmorphia.
  - Damages: $3M compensatory + $3M punitive, split 70% Meta and 30% YouTube.
  - Features at issue included **infinite scroll, autoplay, algorithmic recommendations, beauty filters and push notifications**.
  - Meta and YouTube internal documents were shown to the jury.
- **Caveats:**
  - Post-trial motions were denied, and **Meta is appealing**.
  - One plaintiff, and damages small relative to company size. Its significance is as precedent.
- **Sources:**

### E04 · New Mexico v. Meta: judge declares Meta a public nuisance
- **Tier:** L
- **When:** 2026-08-07
- **What it establishes:**
  - In phase two, the court found Meta created a **"significant public nuisance"** through platforms causing sexual exploitation, interference with education, and adverse mental-health outcomes.
  - Reported as the first time a social media company has been held to be a public nuisance.
  - Additional **$567M**, for **$942M total**. About $420M funds youth mental-health treatment.
  - Ordered design changes for minors:
    - like counts hidden, unless a parent approves showing them
    - push notifications paused from 10pm to 7am
    - use capped at 90 hours a month
  - Coverage reports the court tied endless scroll, autoplay, notifications and recommendations to keeping teens on the platforms longer.
- **Caveats:** Meta says it will appeal.
- **Sources:**

### E05 · Multistate attorneys general v. Meta: the $17B settlement and "the settlement floor"
- **Tier:** S
- **When and where:** Trial opened 2026-08-18 (MDL 3047, N.D. Cal., Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers), settled 2026-08-26.
- **Money:**
  - Up to **$17B over 10 years** to 40+ states, DC and territories.
  - About 70% is unconditional. The rest depends on YouTube, TikTok and Snap becoming subject to comparable obligations.
- **Design terms for teens.** We call these **the settlement floor**:
  - a default **2-hour daily limit** across both apps, liftable only by a parent
  - **no access from midnight to 6am**
  - **notifications muted during school hours** and overnight
  - **a prompt after every 15 minutes of continuous scrolling**
  - **a feed option that is neither ranked by algorithm nor personalized**
  - **autoplay can be turned off**
  - **like counts hidden by default**
  - **cosmetic-surgery and extreme-makeup filters blocked**
  - **private-by-default accounts**
  - **less discoverability by suspicious adult accounts**
  - stronger age detection
  - an independent research foundation
  - third-party audits
- **Caveats:**
  - **Meta continues to deny wrongdoing.**
  - Requires court approval.
  - Applies to teens in participating states only.
- **Sources:**
  - [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/26/nx-s1-5945010/meta-agrees-to-17-billion-settlement-and-new-protections-for-teens)
  - [PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/meta-reaches-17-billion-settlement-with-states-in-landmark-trial-over-teen-social-media-addiction)

### E10 · Engagement-weighted ranking: emoji reactions counted as 5× a like
- **Tier:** D / R
- **When:** reported 2021, practice from 2017
- **What it establishes:**
  - Facebook's ranking weighted emoji reactions, including "angry", at **five times** a like, to boost posts that kept people engaged.
  - Internal researchers found posts drawing angry reactions were disproportionately likely to include misinformation, toxicity and low-quality news.
- **Caveats:**
  - At first *all* emoji reactions were weighted 5×, not only "angry". Facebook later cut the weight of "angry".
- **Sources:**
  - [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2021/10/26/facebook-angry-emoji-algorithm/)
  - [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2021/10/more-internal-documents-show-how-facebooks-algorithm-prioritized-anger-and-posts-that-triggered-it/)

- **Tier:** G
- **When:** 2023-05

### E13 · The science is contested: small average associations
- **Tier:** P
- **When:** 2019
- **What it establishes:** Orben & Przybylski's specification-curve analysis of large adolescent datasets found digital-technology use explains at most 0.4% of the variation in adolescent well-being.
- **Why it matters:** read the harm case as a case about **design and incentives**, rather than "screens are poison".
- **Source:** Orben, A. & Przybylski, A. K. (2019), *Nature Human Behaviour* 3, 173–182. [doi:10.1038/s41562-018-0506-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-018-0506-1)

### E14 · The science is contested: reduction experiments
- **Tier:** P / O
- **When:** 2024
- **What it establishes:**
  - Ferguson's meta-analysis of 27 experiments that reduced social media use found no meaningful average effect on mental health (d ≈ 0.088).
  - Haidt & Rausch argue the analysis was flawed, and that separating the studies differently shows real effects.
- **Caveat:** an open, live dispute, and the manifesto names it openly.
- **Sources:**
  - Ferguson, C. J. (2024), "Do social media experiments prove a link with mental health," *Psychology of Popular Media*. [doi:10.1037/ppm0000541](https://doi.org/10.1037/ppm0000541)
  - Rebuttal: [After Babel](https://www.afterbabel.com/p/fundamental-flaws-part-3)

### E18 · Abuse by people close to you, through ordinary features
- **Tier:** P
- **When:** 2018
- **What it establishes:**
  - A study of 89 participants found abusers in intimate relationships mostly use **ordinary app features rather than hacking** to monitor, impersonate, harass and threaten. The authors call this a "UI-bound adversary".

### E21 · Close ties and composed messages lift well-being
- **Tier:** P (by Facebook's own researchers)
- **When:** 2016
- **What it establishes:**
  - In 1,910 users, with surveys linked to server logs, well-being improved with **composed, targeted communication from strong ties**.
  - One-click feedback (likes) and passive reading of broadcasts did not show that benefit, and composed messages from weak ties didn't either.
- **Source:** Burke, M. & Kraut, R. E. (2016), "The Relationship Between Facebook Use and Well-Being Depends on Communication Type and Tie Strength," *Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication* 21(4), 265–281. [doi:10.1111/jcc4.12162](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcc4.12162)

<a id="E22"></a>
### E22 · A prompt before posting reduces offensive content
- **Tier:** P
- **When:** 2022
- **What it establishes:** a randomized experiment on Twitter.
  - Prompted users posted **6% fewer offensive tweets**.
  - Prompted users cancelled 9% of the time and revised 22% of the time.
  - Effects persisted: fewer future offensive tweets and fewer offensive replies received.
- **Source:** Katsaros, M., Yang, K. & Fratamico, L. (2022), "Reconsidering Tweets," *ICWSM*. [arXiv:2112.00773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00773)

<a id="E23"></a>
### E23 · Bridging-based ranking
- **Tier:** P (preprint) / O
- **When:** 2023
- **What it establishes:** ranking can reward content that earns approval *across* groups that usually disagree, instead of raw engagement. This counters divisiveness without judging any viewpoint.
- **Sources:**
  - Ovadya, A. & Thorburn, L. (2023), "Bridging Systems." [arXiv:2301.09976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09976)
  - [Harvard Ash Center policy brief](https://ash.harvard.edu/resources/bridging-based-ranking)

<a id="E24"></a>
### E24 · Community by Design (formerly "Prosocial Media")
- **Tier:** P (preprint)
- **When:** 2025, revised 2026
- **What it establishes:**
  - A platform model that makes "the social fabric an explicit output as well as input".
  - It organizes people into communities and rewards content that **bridges** (consensus across internal diversity) and **balances** (fair representation of disagreement).
  - Advertisers target communities only with consent.
- **Source:** Weyl, E. G., Thorburn, L., de Keulenaar, E., Mchangama, J., Siddarth, D. & Tang, A. [arXiv:2502.10834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10834)

### E26 · USC Neely Center: Design Code for Social Media
- **Tier:** O
- **What it establishes:**
  - Nine minimum, **content-neutral** design standards, including:
    - better defaults to protect children
    - optimizing for users' **expressed preferences** rather than engagement

- **Tier:** P

### E30 · Harvard Study of Adult Development
- **Tier:** P / O
- **When:** since 1938
- **What it establishes:** the longest-running study of adult life finds the warmth of close relationships is among the strongest predictors of health and happiness in later life. The people most satisfied in their relationships at 50 were the healthiest at 80, and relationship satisfaction at 50 predicted later health better than cholesterol did.
- **Sources:**
  - Waldinger, R. & Schulz, M. (2023), *The Good Life*. Simon & Schuster.
  - [Study site](https://www.adultdevelopmentstudy.org/)
  - [Harvard Gazette (2017)](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2017/04/over-nearly-80-years-harvard-study-has-been-showing-how-to-live-a-healthy-and-happy-life/)

### E46 · Friends across income lines and children's prospects
- **Tier:** P
- **When:** 2022
- **What it establishes:** using 21 million people's friendship networks, "economic connectedness" (how many
  friends people with lower incomes have among people with higher incomes) is one of the strongest predictors
  of upward mobility yet found. If children from low-income homes grew up in counties as connected as the
  average higher-income child's, their adult incomes would rise by about 20% on average.
- **Source:** Chetty, R. et al. (2022), "Social capital I: measurement and associations with economic
  mobility," *Nature* 608: 108–121. [doi:10.1038/s41586-022-04996-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-04996-4)

<a id="E47"></a>
### E47 · Social ties and the common cold
- **Tier:** P
- **When:** 1997
- **What it establishes:** healthy volunteers were given a cold virus. Those with the fewest kinds of social
  ties (1 to 3, out of 12 such as family, friends, work, neighbours and community groups) were about 4.2 times
  as likely to develop a cold as those with 6 or more, and risk fell steadily as the mix of ties grew.
- **Source:** Cohen, S. et al. (1997), "Social ties and susceptibility to the common cold," *JAMA* 277(24):
  1940–1944. [doi:10.1001/jama.1997.03540480040036](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.1997.03540480040036)

<a id="E48"></a>
### E48 · Happiness and meaningful conversation
- **Tier:** P
- **When:** 2010, replicated 2018
- **What it establishes:** people wore a small recorder that sampled their days. The happiest had about twice
  as many substantive conversations as the least happy, spent about 25% less time alone and about 70% more
  time talking. A larger 2018 replication confirmed the links with time alone, time talking and substantive
  conversation (it did not confirm the original finding about small talk).
- **Sources:**
  - Mehl, M. R. et al. (2010), "Eavesdropping on happiness," *Psychological Science* 21(4): 539–541.
    [doi:10.1177/0956797610362675](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797610362675)
  - Milek, A. et al. (2018), "'Eavesdropping on happiness' revisited," *Psychological Science* 29(9).
    [doi:10.1177/0956797618774252](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797618774252)

### E50 · Posting community rules
- **Tier:** P
- **When:** 2019
- **What it establishes:** in a randomized field experiment across 2,190 discussions in a 13.5-million-member
  science community, showing the rules to newcomers raised their rule compliance by more than 8 percentage
  points and their participation by 70% on average. Visible norms shaped both behaviour and who chose to join.
- **Source:** Matias, J. N. (2019), "Preventing harassment and increasing group participation through social
  norms in 2,190 online science discussions," *PNAS* 116(20). [doi:10.1073/pnas.1813486116](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1813486116)

- **Tier:** P

- **Tier:** P / O
