# What changed

The manifesto is a draft, and it changes in public. This page records every substantive change, so anyone can
see what was revised and why. To propose the next one, use the [feedback page](https://flocialmanifesto.org/feedback).

---

## Draft v0.2 (open for review)

Shaped by the first readers' feedback before the public launch.

### For every kind of community
- **Wherever people gather.** A new section shows Flocial applying to clubs, teams and schools, congregations
  and nonprofits, workplaces, businesses and their customers, cities and public bodies, any app one feature at
  a time, and families. Flocial is a set of commitments, not a product.
- **A day across many communities.** The examples now move through a book club and golf group, a workplace, a
  bike shop's customers, a youth team, a city consultation and the last post of the day.
- **Principles widened.**
  - Who sees a post is chosen (4).
  - Strangers can't message unless invited (5).
  - Gatherings of every kind count as success (7).
  - Location is off by default (10, 12).
  - The coming-of-age review covers whatever family, school or team posted about a child (13).
  - Value is shared where a community earns it (15).
  - Outside organizations come by invitation (VI, 19).
- **Organizers.** The people a community trusts to look after it. Each platform can name the role its own way.
- **The Community Charter** replaces the Values Charter, adding how members treat each other. Checking outside
  organizations against it is now an optional part.

### Said in the affirmative
- **The case for flocial networks comes first.** The court record and research moved to an
  [appendix](EVIDENCE.md). Nothing on the home page names a company.
- **Flourishing over attention** replaces "flourishing over engagement". Engagement is welcome when the person is
  the one who benefits.
- **Money as values.** The principles state values and leave business models to builders.
  - Plain prices and no hidden costs (14).
  - The community shares in the value it creates (15).
  - People stay because it's worth staying (16).
  - Standing is earned by conduct (20).
- **Outcomes over implementations.** The promises describe what people get, and leave how it's built to
  builders.
- **An AI assistant** that acts only when a person taps (11).
- **A plainer voice.** Fewer rhetorical contrasts, semicolons and colons, and promises phrased in the
  affirmative.

### Protection
- **Built for every age:** readable type and simple steps, help from someone you trust without handing over
  your account, and extra care against the scams aimed at older adults.
- **When the host has power** (an employer, a school, a government), privacy from the host is stronger, and
  members help run the space.
- **A seal names what it covers**, a product or a feature, never a whole company.

### Evidence
- From 45 to 57 sources, including research on posted community rules, self-governing communities, what makes
  teams effective, workplace monitoring and interruptions, the right to disconnect, consensus-seeking public consultation, peer support
  communities, and fraud against older adults. Every source states its limits.

### Why community matters
- **Four numbers across the wider scope:** a community (posted norms), a workplace (safety to speak up), a city
  (common ground in a public consultation) and a whole life (happiest at 50, healthiest at 80).

### Signing
- **Four ways to appear** on the signer wall: by name, by first name and initial, by a short description, or
  privately. Everyone counts.
- **Less kept.** After a signature is confirmed, only a one-way fingerprint of the email remains, to stop
  duplicates.
