# The Flocial Manifesto

### What online community can be

*Draft v0.1 · October 2026 · Open for comment · [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/)*

---

## A new word

**flocial** */ˈfloʊ.ʃəl/* (adj.). Describes a network that measures its success by whether its members
**flourish**, not by how much of their attention it holds.

It comes from *flourishing* + *social*, with a little of *flock* in it, for people who belong to each other and
move together.

"Flourishing" here is more than a mood. It's something that can be measured. The largest study of it ever run
follows 200,000 people in 22 countries across six domains [[E28]](EVIDENCE.md#E28):
- happiness
- health
- meaning
- character
- **close relationships**
- financial stability

A flocial network is built to strengthen those six domains and is willing to be measured on them.

---

## Wherever people gather

Flocial is a set of commitments, not a product. Build a whole network on it, or bring it to a single feature
and adopt the principles that touch what you build. Some places it fits:

- **Clubs and friend groups.** Plans for the book club or the golf four that turn into meeting up.
- **Teams and schools.** Photos of children seen only by their own families, and quiet nights.
- **Congregations and nonprofits.** Members come first, and volunteers are thanked for their time.
- **Workplaces.** The decisions that matter, easy to find. Nobody is graded by how often they post
  [[E53]](EVIDENCE.md#E53).
- **Businesses and their customers.** Real answers from real people, and nothing sold unasked
  [[E56]](EVIDENCE.md#E56).
- **Cities and public bodies.** The views neighbours agree on rise to the top [[E55]](EVIDENCE.md#E55).
- **Any app, one feature at a time.** Chat, comments or notifications, built the flocial way into what you
  already run.
- **Families.** A private place for the people who matter most.

## What online community can be

Picture an ordinary day across the communities you belong to.

- **Morning.** Nothing pinged overnight. Over coffee, one short summary says the book club picked next month's
  novel and the golf group moved Saturday's tee time to 8.
- **Mid-morning.** At work, the team space opens on yesterday's three decisions, then ends. Nobody is ranked
  by how much they post, so the quiet expert speaks up too.
- **Midday.** You ask your bike shop's community about a squeaky brake. A mechanic answers, another rider
  adds a tip, and nobody tries to sell you anything.
- **Afternoon.** The coach shares photos from today's game. Each child appears only to their own family, until
  they're old enough to choose for themselves.
- **Early evening.** Your city asks about new bike lanes. The views you see first are the ones neighbours on
  both sides agree with. You add yours.
- **Night.** Fourteen new posts across your groups, all seen, and Saturday's block party is set. You put the
  phone down.

None of this is science fiction. Each moment comes from a principle below, and each is possible with the
technology we already have.

### Why community matters
- **Shared norms open the door.** When a large community posted its norms where newcomers could see them,
  70% more newcomers joined the conversation [[E50]](EVIDENCE.md#E50).
- **Teams thrive when people can speak up.** Across 180 of Google's teams, feeling safe to speak up mattered
  more than anything else [[E57]](EVIDENCE.md#E57).
- **Cities can find common ground.** In Taiwan, more than 4,500 citizens found consensus on a divisive law, on
  a platform that showed agreement first [[E55]](EVIDENCE.md#E55).
- **It pays off for life.** The people happiest in their relationships at 50 were the healthiest at 80
  [[E30]](EVIDENCE.md#E30), and people with strong relationships are about 50% more likely to survive over
  the study periods [[E29]](EVIDENCE.md#E29).

## The principles

Seven pillars and twenty-two promises describe what you can count on in a flocial network. Each one is backed
by evidence and can be checked, and each comes with a picture of what it looks like in practice.

### I. Time: give attention back

**1. Every feed ends.**
The feed has an end you can reach, and videos wait for you to press play. Leaving is always one easy step
[[E01]](EVIDENCE.md#E01) [[E05]](EVIDENCE.md#E05).
*In practice:* Open it and you see what’s new since you last looked, eleven posts from the people you follow. After the eleventh, that’s everything, so you can put the phone down without wondering what you missed.

**2. Quiet by default.**
- Notifications say *who* and *where*, and leave the *what* for when you open them.
- They arrive together in a few calm batches and go silent at night [[E04]](EVIDENCE.md#E04).
- The same holds at work, where constant interruptions raise stress [[E53]](EVIDENCE.md#E53) and a growing
  number of countries give workers a legal right to disconnect [[E54]](EVIDENCE.md#E54).

*In practice:* An alert says “Dee posted in Garden Club” and leaves her words for when you open it. After bedtime everything waits for morning, and the rest gathers into a Sunday digest.

**3. In order, or by the people you choose.**
- The default is newest first.
- Where ranking is truly needed, rank by what people *say* they want, or by what earns agreement across
  divides [[E23]](EVIDENCE.md#E23) [[E26]](EVIDENCE.md#E26). Ranking by whatever makes people react most is
  exactly what a flocial network leaves behind [[E10]](EVIDENCE.md#E10).
- In a public consultation, ranking for agreement has helped thousands of citizens find common ground that
  became law [[E55]](EVIDENCE.md#E55).

*In practice:* Newest first, and every post gets the same chance to be seen.

### II. Closeness: close ties come first

**4. Closeness over audience.**
- You choose who sees each post, from one small group to the whole community.
- Reach is no measure of success here, and nobody keeps a public popularity score.
- Built for the message written to someone close, the kind of communication that actually raises
  well-being [[E21]](EVIDENCE.md#E21) [[E31]](EVIDENCE.md#E31).

*In practice:* Each post goes to the group it was meant for. There are no follower counts and no public tallies of likes.

**5. People introduce people.**
- Strangers can't message you unless you open the door.
- Newcomers arrive through someone already there, in that person's own words, and communities connect only
  when both ask to [[E03]](EVIDENCE.md#E03) [[E05]](EVIDENCE.md#E05).

*In practice:* You decide who can message you. Someone new joins a group when a member introduces them, in their own words, and “No thanks” is final.

**6. Pause before sending, and disagree in private.**
A short pause before a heated message measurably reduces cruelty, and the effect lasts
[[E22]](EVIDENCE.md#E22). Conflict deserves a private room where people can hear each other.

*In practice:* Before a reply sends, there’s a moment to reread it. Disagreements stay between the people involved, and everyone has their say before anyone answers. Abuse goes to real help, never to mediation.

**7. Success is measured off-screen.**
The best outcome of a flocial network is a phone call or a visit, and those are what to count
[[E21]](EVIDENCE.md#E21) [[E29]](EVIDENCE.md#E29).
*In practice:* The proudest moment is a nudge to put the phone down and meet up, with simple tools that turn a plan into a tee time or a book club night.

### III. Dignity and safety: protection without watching

**Built for every age.** Readable type and simple steps, help from someone you trust without handing over your
account, and extra care against the scams aimed at older adults, who lose more to online fraud than any other
age group [[E49]](EVIDENCE.md#E49).

**When the host has power.** Where the host has power over its members, as an employer, a school or a
government does, privacy from the host is stronger. Monitored workers report more stress and poorer mental
health [[E52]](EVIDENCE.md#E52). Members also help run the space, because communities last when the people they
serve help make the rules and the people who enforce them answer to members [[E51]](EVIDENCE.md#E51).

**8. Smallest circle by default, with consent you can see and take back.**
- Information stays in the context it was shared in [[E32]](EVIDENCE.md#E32).
- Sharing more widely is a choice, recorded and reversible.

*In practice:* Only the people you chose can see a post, wherever it travels, and “only me” stays private even from the people who host it. Health, money, travel and anything about a child start in the smallest circle.

**9. People name people.**
- Software identifies only people who said yes for themselves, and children not at all.
- Naming someone in a photo keeps it with the same audience.

*In practice:* “People in your community name people.” A tag keeps a photo with the same audience, and anyone can quietly take themselves out of one.

**10. Safety includes safety from the people closest to you.**
Most harm through technology comes through ordinary features [[E18]](EVIDENCE.md#E18). A community built on
close ties protects people from those closest to them when it has to, with:
- quiet exits
- blocking that nobody is notified about
- hiding from a shared space, such as a group
- routes to real help

*In practice:* Quiet removal from photos, blocking anyone without them being told, routes to crisis lines, and location that is off by default and never live.

**11. AI serves human connection and never stands in for it.**
- AI drafts and people decide. It acts only when a person taps.
- It always says it's a machine.
- No one's voice or likeness is simulated without their consent. No one who has died is "brought back"
  unless they agreed while they were alive [[E15]](EVIDENCE.md#E15) [[E35]](EVIDENCE.md#E35).

*In practice:* An AI assistant drafts, and posts, sends or shares only when you tap. It says it’s a machine, and it recommends things only when asked.

### IV. Children: safe by design, growing into their own story

**12. Protection built in, without surveillance.**
- The safest settings are the default for children [[E19]](EVIDENCE.md#E19) [[E20]](EVIDENCE.md#E20).
- Guardians get the oversight a child's safety needs and no more [[E06]](EVIDENCE.md#E06).

*In practice:* Guardians see who a child talks to, and the conversation stays the child’s. A child’s location stays private, and children see no offers.

**13. Children grow into their own story.**
- Children didn't choose what was posted about them [[E33]](EVIDENCE.md#E33).
- They have rights to take part online as well as to be protected [[E34]](EVIDENCE.md#E34).
- As they come of age, they review what family, school or team posted about them, and decide what stays.

*In practice:* At 18, a young person sees everything posted about them by family, school or team, and decides what stays.

### V. Fairness: shared value

**14. The people are the customer.**
- Revenue comes from serving members well.
- How the community is funded is stated plainly, and every cost is visible up front.
- No one is priced out of belonging.

*In practice:* Prices are plain and up front. Every listing says what the community earns from it, and anyone who’s invited can take part fully, whatever they can pay.

**15. The community shares in the value it creates.**
- When value is created together, it flows back to the community as a whole, in money or in kind
  [[E36]](EVIDENCE.md#E36).
- The benefit is shared by everyone, so friendships stay friendships.
- Offers follow what people *say* they want [[E38]](EVIDENCE.md#E38).

*In practice:* Where a community earns, a share comes back to it, and members decide together how it’s used. A customer community might share in discounts or have a say in what’s made.

**16. Leaving is as easy as joining.**
- Cancelling takes as few steps as signing up [[E17]](EVIDENCE.md#E17).
- Your history comes with you [[E37]](EVIDENCE.md#E37).
- People stay because it's worth staying.

*In practice:* Your posts and history stay yours, whatever you pay. Leaving takes one step, and everything comes with you, ready to use elsewhere.

**17. Promises outlive the company.**
- Users' rights to their data and to leave survive any change of ownership.
- Control is structured to protect the mission [[E44]](EVIDENCE.md#E44).
- There is a published plan for shutting down, so everyone's memories are kept safe.

*In practice:* A legal structure that locks in the mission, user rights written to survive a sale, and a published plan for shutting down.

### VI. Open doors: the wider world, on the community's terms

**18. Each community writes its charter.**
- Every community, from a book club to a congregation, can write a short
  [**Community Charter**](COMMUNITY-CHARTER.md) saying who it's for, how members treat each other and what it
  values.
- People and any AI assistant the community uses can read it.
- Posting it helps. When a large community showed its rules to newcomers, their participation rose by 70%
  [[E50]](EVIDENCE.md#E50).
- It is never sold and never used to target anyone [[E38]](EVIDENCE.md#E38).

*In practice:* A one-page charter covers who the community is for, how members treat each other, what it won’t accept and who can change it. Anything new suggested to the community is held up against it.

**19. Outside voices come in by invitation.**
- When a network brings outside organizations in, such as brands, newsrooms, causes or apps, they come *by the
  community's invitation*.
- The community can mute or remove any of them at any time.
- We rate how an organization *behaves*, using standards that already exist [[E43]](EVIDENCE.md#E43), and
  leave what it believes to each community [[E25]](EVIDENCE.md#E25).

**20. Standing is earned by conduct.**
- Providers are checked against an open, evidence-based rubric, with disqualifying practices first and grades
  after.
- Every grade links to its evidence, and every provider can appeal.
- Close scores share visibility fairly [[E39]](EVIDENCE.md#E39).
- Scores reflect conduct alone, and any paid relationship is disclosed [[E41]](EVIDENCE.md#E41).
- Conduct is verified, because signing a pledge proves nothing on its own [[E42]](EVIDENCE.md#E42).

### VII. Honesty: tell the truth, including about yourself

**21. Publish what you learn, harms included.**
- If your research finds that your product hurts people, publish it and fix it
  [[E08]](EVIDENCE.md#E08).
- Publish a yearly **Flocial Report** on how well members are flourishing, including what went wrong.

**22. Let others check.**
- Give independent researchers safe access to the data.
- Accept independent audits [[E05]](EVIDENCE.md#E05).
- Protect the people inside who speak up.
- The steward of this manifesto doesn't certify its own products [[E40]](EVIDENCE.md#E40).

*In practice:* An independent steward scores how it’s run, on the open rubric, in public, and researchers get safe access to study it.

## What we value

**We flourish together.** Seven values sit underneath the principles.

- **Flourishing over attention.** We want people to engage in ways that leave them better off. Success is
  measured in how their lives go, from closer ties to better health.
- **Closeness over audience.** Your words go to the people who matter to you, and nobody keeps score.
- **Time returned over time spent.** A good community gives you back to your day, then waits quietly until you
  need it.
- **Consent over collection.** Nothing about you is gathered, shared or shown unless you said yes, and you can
  change your mind.
- **Conduct over clout.** Businesses and organizations earn their place by how they behave.
- **Shared value over extracted value.** When the community creates value, everyone in it shares in it.
- **An ending over an endless feed.** Every feed has a last post. Then you go live your life.

## Why now

Many of us have felt the pull of a feed that never ends, or an evening online that left us lonelier than we
started. Loneliness now touches about half of American adults [[E12]](EVIDENCE.md#E12).

In 2026, courts, state attorneys general and researchers documented what many people had sensed. Some of the
most-used platforms were designed in ways that kept people scrolling at a real cost, especially to young
people [[E01]](EVIDENCE.md#E01) [[E03]](EVIDENCE.md#E03) [[E05]](EVIDENCE.md#E05). The same year, a
settlement set new protections for teen accounts, including hidden like counts and quiet nights
[[E05]](EVIDENCE.md#E05).

We see that as a floor to build on. A flocial network offers all of it to everyone, by default, without
anyone being watched more closely to get it [[E06]](EVIDENCE.md#E06), and then goes further.

The science of how much average screen time affects the average person is still debated
[[E13]](EVIDENCE.md#E13) [[E14]](EVIDENCE.md#E14). Our case rests on something simpler. Online communities can
be built for people to flourish, and here is how.

The full record of every case, settlement and study is in the [appendix](EVIDENCE.md).
